# How the price was set

$5 for each message that actually sends. Printing and postage on letters at cost. Free to try, and free for Qloo customers.

The method is three questions. What does it cost us? What is it worth to you? Who should keep the difference?

Measured numbers are from our own database and bills on 30 Oct 2026. Outside benchmarks are at the bottom, and most come from sales-tool vendors, so treat them as ranges.

## 1. What to charge for

A desk does its work whether or not you use it. Most cards shouldn't go out, and that's the point of the approval step. So a monthly fee would charge you for cards you rejected, and a small artist's team would pay the same as Live Nation.

The fair unit is a message you approved that really left. No seats, no minimum. Drafts, rejects, holds and simulations cost nothing, and bounces don't count. A small team sends a little and pays a little.

## 2. The floor: what a send costs us

| | Measured |
|---|---|
| Models per card | Under $1. Every Google Cloud bill this year (EUR 441.95 gross, including all testing) over 614 cards filed is EUR 0.72 |
| Email | Close to nothing, a normal mailbox |
| Letter | $1.06 first class, $6.94 certified, passed through at cost |
| Qloo calls per card | About 10 with cache, 40 to 60 on a brand-new desk |

Qloo doesn't publish a price, so that last line has no dollar figure yet. It's the biggest unknown here. See the data test below.

## 3. The ceiling: what the work is worth

A card replaces finding the right partner, finding who decides, and writing and checking the pitch. Benchmarks put that at 20 to 45 minutes an account, for a rep costing $110k to $160k a year (2,080 hours). A person still reads each card before approving, so we take 2 minutes off.

| | Minutes saved | Hourly cost | Worth per send | You keep at $5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 18 | $53 | $16 | 68% |
| Middle | 28 | $65 | $30 | 83% |
| High | 43 | $77 | $55 | 91% |

## 4. The split

Most of the value should stay with the person sending. We set a rule first: you keep at least two thirds, even on the low estimate. That caps the price at about $5.30. We rounded down to $5.

That leaves about $4 a send over the model cost, which is enough to run this.

## 5. Four tests

**Against hiring.** An SDR costs $9,200 to $13,300 a month and books 15 to 25 meetings, so $370 to $890 a meeting. Cold email books a meeting from 0.5% to 2% of sends. At $5 that's $250 to $1,000 a meeting, and $500 at 1%. It beats a hire from about 1% up. It's close, not a landslide.

**Against mass email.** A bulk tool can get a meeting for about $150. We lose this one on purpose. Desks send five emails a day to people chosen one at a time. If you want volume, buy a mailer.

**Against an agent.** Agents take 10% to 20% of the deals they bring in. We take no cut. 200 sends costs $1,000, which is the commission on a $5,000 to $10,000 deal.

**Data.** The $4 margin covers Qloo up to about 6 cents a call on a brand-new desk. If a licence costs more, this price has to change for people who aren't Qloo customers.

## Why Qloo customers get it free

They've already paid for the data, which is the part we can't do without. On their own key, the biggest unknown above goes away. Every desk also puts Qloo's number into a real deal, which gives them more reason to keep using it. That's worth more to us than $5 a send. They still pay printing and postage on letters, because that money goes to the print house.

## What we don't know yet

Reply rate. Almost nothing has been sent for real yet, so the meeting rates above are other people's. We'll look at this price again after the first 200 real sends, or as soon as Qloo gives us a rate.
